Pricing Lawn Care by Square Footage vs. by Time

Ask five lawn care operators how they price a job and you'll get two main answers: by square footage, or "by feel," which usually means an unspoken time estimate anyway. Both have a real place. Neither works alone.

What square footage gets right

A per-thousand-square-feet rate is fast to quote, easy for a client to understand, and roughly correlates with mowing time on a flat, obstacle-free lot. For a quick phone or drive-by quote, it's a reasonable starting point.

What it misses

Square footage says nothing about slope, trim complexity, gate access, tree cover, or how overgrown a yard gets between visits. Two 6,000-square-foot lots can take 20 minutes and 50 minutes respectively, and a footage-only price treats them identically. That gap is where margin quietly disappears — not on any one job, but across dozens of them, every week, all season.

Why time is the tiebreaker

Square footage is a reasonable way to set an opening quote. Tracked time is what tells you whether that quote was right, three weeks in. Once you have real minutes per visit, you can see exactly which "average" lots are actually taking twice as long as the footage estimate assumed — and reprice those specifically, instead of guessing at a blanket rate increase across everyone.

Quote by footage. Verify — and correct — with tracked time.
