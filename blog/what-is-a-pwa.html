What Is a PWA, and Why Should You Care?

PWA stands for Progressive Web App — a website that behaves like an installed app. No app store, no download wait, and it still works when you've got zero bars. For a one-person crew running everything from a phone, that combination matters more than the buzzword suggests.

No app store fees, no gatekeeping

Apple and Google both take a cut of app-store purchases, and both make you clear a review process before anything reaches your phone — including updates. A PWA skips all of it. You open a link, and the current version is already there. A bug fix ships the moment it's deployed, not whenever a store review finishes.

Zero installation wait

Installing a PWA is: open the link, tap "Add to Home Screen," done. No multi-hundred-megabyte download, no waiting on a slow connection at a job site, no storage warning on an older phone. It's on your home screen in under a minute, and it looks and behaves like any other app icon.

Offline in rural dead zones

This is the part that actually matters for fieldwork: a well-built PWA can run without a signal. It stores what it needs locally, keeps working while you're in a dip with no bars, and syncs everything the moment you're back in range. A regular website just shows an error with no connection. A PWA built offline-first doesn't notice the difference.

Why it fits a trade business specifically

  • Works on any phone — old Android, new iPhone, doesn't matter
  • No account approval delays — you're using it the same day you find it
  • Updates you don't have to think about — there's no "update available" nag screen

That's the whole reason MowClock is a PWA and not an app-store app: it's the fastest, cheapest way to get real software into a working person's pocket.
